Digital Modernization of Bus Driver Allocation

IEMS 394: Engineering Capstone Project

This project, currently in progress as part of my engineering degree's capstone, is all about digital modernization. Our client is a large bus contractor company that serves school districts all over the U.S. The goal of the project is to convert the currently manual process for assigning bus drivers to routes into a more efficient digital way. Our current solution is a self-standing web-app, coded entirely in Python, that utilizes the Gale-Shapley allocation algorithm to assign drivers.

Scrambling for Power: The Impact of Sudden Reactor Shutdowns on Short-Run Electricity Prices

ECON 383: Applied Econometrics Final Project

I study the short-run impact of unexpected nuclear reactor outages (scrams) on electricity prices by implementing a stacked Regression Discontinuity in Time (RDiT) design across multiple plants and electricity markets. Focusing on 13 nuclear scrams across 10 sites, I align each event on its own outage timestamp and estimate the instantaneous price jump per megawatt of lost capacity. According to my estimates, each 1 GW of nuclear capacity lost raises the zonal marginal price by $26.18 ± 15.06/MWh. See the proposal and presentation for more!

Power Up: How Policymakers Can Save the Grid

Stanford Economic Review

One of the most pressing challenges facing us today is how to modernize the U.S. power grid while balancing the twin concerns of energy security and decarbonization. My commentary "Power Up: How Policymakers Can Save the Grid", published in the Stanford Economic Review, looks at how different levels of government can cut red tape to harness the trillions in public and private investment dollars to accelerate the conversion of the American grid.

Deterministic and Stochastic Food Routing and Allocation

IEMS 365: Analytics for Social Good Final Project

This project examined the case of the Food Bank of the Southern Tier (FBST) in upstate New York. FBST has a set of mobile food pantries that are routed throughout the various cities of the region. The goal of this project was to develop a schedule for these mobile pantries to maximize the effectiveness of FBST. Our analysis included four parts: demographic analysis, deterministic allocation, cold-weather routing and an Eisenberg-Gale program for stochastic allocation. See the final report, the final presentation and the GitHub repo (link WIP) for the results and methodology.

International Semiconductor R&D Capabilities Mapping

U.S. Department Of State

This report utilizes open-source data from academic journals and government patent offices to construct a global map of semiconductor assets, institutions and linkages. By leveraging this data, we were able to construct an interactive dashboard, allowing users to filter by various levels, countries and topics. Additionally, we provide a report and presentation with analytics on key regional clusters and the global state of play with respect to various sub-fields. The dashboard, code and data files are all available on GitHub.
